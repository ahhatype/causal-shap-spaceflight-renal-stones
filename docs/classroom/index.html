Credit and effect are different quantities.

A coalition attribution averages over contexts in which other variables are already set. A do(X) contrast lets the mediator respond. They need not give X the same number.

φX = ½[v(X) − v(∅)] + ½[v(X,M) − v(M)]. φM uses the reverse order. Both sum to f(x,m) − E[f].

In the structural game, do(X=x) updates M, but a coalition that also sets M fixes it at m. These are symmetric two-player Shapley values; the paper's DAG-asymmetric prototype uses a different ordering restriction.

02 / Challenge the assumption

Direct does not mean linear.

A curved direct cause

Let Y = X² + e. The edge X → Y is direct. Changing X from −1 to 1 has zero mean effect; changing it from 0 to 2 has effect +4. The intervention range matters.

Two indistinguishable directions

Linear Gaussian worlds X → Y and Y → X can have the same observed distribution. Setting X changes Y in one and leaves it unchanged in the other. Linearity alone cannot identify direction.

Depth also needs qualifications. A single path with |a|<1 at every link attenuates. Multiple paths may add or cancel. This is why the nonlinear and noise experiments should test an assumption, not illustrate a supposed law.
